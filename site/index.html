1 · The meaning map

An embedding turns a piece of text into a point in space. Similar meanings land close together, even when the words are completely different. Here's a hand-drawn toy version of that map.

Click anywhere on the map to drop a query vector

cooking nursing notes chess privacy

Hover a dot to see the "text" it stands for. Click to drop a query embedding: the dashed lines show a search finding its three nearest neighbors. This is, mechanically, everything embeddings-based retrieval is.

Where the picture is a lie (on purpose): real embeddings are hundreds of dimensions, not two, and the "distance" is usually cosine similarity on unit-length vectors, not straight-line distance. But the reading of the picture — similar meaning = nearby points, retrieval = grab the neighbors — transfers exactly. Chapter 1 in the handbook explains what the extra dimensions buy you.

What to notice: when both vectors are length 1 (which is how embedding models ship), cosine and dot product give the same number — that's why libraries use them interchangeably. Euclidean distance moves in the opposite direction of the scores: near-identical vectors have a tiny distance. If your search results "look inverted," this is usually why.

4 · Chunking, live

You can't embed a whole document usefully — one vector per forty pages is an average, and averages erase. So we cut documents into chunks at natural seams (headings, paragraphs), embed each chunk, and retrieve the few best. Chunk quality is where RAG lives or dies.

Drag the slider and watch the seams

What to notice: at small sizes, chunks become orphans — fragments like "Items must be unused and…" that carry almost no meaning on their own, so they can't match any query well. At huge sizes, chunks cover several topics at once, so they're only moderately similar to everything and rank last. The sweet spot is typically 400–1200 characters, one topic per chunk, with a little overlap. Chapter 4 is entirely about getting this right.

Dimension hygiene: all vectors in one collection must come from the same model. nomic-embed-text is 768-dimensional; mxbai-embed-large is 1024. Mixing them in one store is the #1 way to get confidently wrong results. Write your model name into the metadata of every batch. Future-you will send flowers.

6 · Is RAG overkill? Ask the flow

Most of the time you don't need RAG, and building it anyway buys you silent failure modes. Answer two questions:

The one-line heuristic: try to answer your question with Ctrl+F first. If you can — no embeddings. RAG is for corpora that are too big, too scattered, worded in ways you can't predict, or growing too fast to index by hand. Everything else is infrastructure cosplay. (Chapter 6.)
